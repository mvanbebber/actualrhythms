Jazz rhythm reading

Read rhythm like the players who invented it.

Nearly 20,000 of the rhythms in Actual Rhythms come from 586 transcribed jazz solos, heads and arrangements, recorded between 1925 and 2009. Read a phrase from Armstrong, Parker or Coltrane, tap it, then play it at the tempo of the record.

Start with a solo The players

Turn on Jazz under Repertoire in Settings to read only from the solos.

Written like a chart

Solos are notated straight, the way a lead sheet or big-band chart writes them: you read eighths and swing them. Triplets stay triplets, and every beat is spelled so you can see where it falls.

At the tempo of the record

Press Original next to the tempo slider and the excerpt plays at the recording's average tempo, from a 60 BPM ballad to a burning up-tempo chorus. Or slow it down and work up.

From the Weimar Jazz Database

Most of the solos are expert transcriptions from the Jazzomat Research Project at the University of Music Franz Liszt Weimar. Each excerpt names the tune, the player, the instrument, the bars and the year.

Charlie Parker, note for note

59 Parker heads and solos from Charlie Parker 60 Melodies and Solos.

From Red Cross in 1944 to Confirmation in 1953: Ko Ko, Donna Lee, Billie's Bounce, Chasin' the Bird, Scrapple from the Apple, Yardbird Suite and more, transcribed from the recordings by Michael Van Bebber for his book Charlie Parker 60 Melodies and Solos. Each excerpt says whether it comes from the head or the solo, and names the studio, the date and the record it was issued on.

Original plays each one at the tempo Bird took it, from 165 BPM on Billie's Bounce to 308 on Ko Ko.

Michael Van Bebber's other transcriptions add 104 more jazz recordings: John Coltrane on Moment's Notice, Oleo and Mr. P.C., Clifford Brown, Freddie Hubbard, Kenny Dorham, Lee Morgan, Thelonious Monk, Horace Silver's quintets and Basie's band, with every part labeled, from the horn lines to the bass and drums. 32 soul, funk, pop and rock songs, Stevie Wonder to Khruangbin, are under Songs.
