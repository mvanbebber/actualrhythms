Same rhythm, different notation

Michael Jackson plays a Chinese folk song.

The bass line of Off the Wall has the rhythm of Mai wawa, a folk song from Shaanxi, note for note: the record bars it in 4/4, the song in 2/4. Only one other piece in the corpus has it, another Chinese folk song. Actual Rhythms finds matches like this across all 11,896 pieces, so every excerpt can show you its twins.

Same notes, another meter

Identical on the page, a different time signature.

Nothing changes but the meter. In 2/2 the beat is the half note; in 4/4 it is the quarter. Read both and feel where the pulse moves.

Bach, Joplin and Gershwin

The bass of a Bach chorale is a line Gershwin wrote for They All Laughed. Joplin has it too, at half the values.

Charlie Parker and an Irish reel

Bebop triplets and a fiddler's ornament triplets land in the same places. Only the meter differs.

Cannonball Adderley, Beethoven and a folk song from Shanxi

Three continents and three meters: Zawinul's tune in 4/4, Beethoven's viola in 2/2, and the folk song in 2/4 at half the values.

Half or double the note values

Same rhythm, different note values.

Halve every note and a bar of 4/4 becomes a bar of 2/4. Double them and two bars become four. The rhythm doesn't change; only how busy it looks.

Charlie Parker and Jingle Bells

The riff of Now's the Time is Jingle Bells at half the note values. Mozart's "Hunt" Quartet, Beethoven and three folk songs from Germany, Austria and the Baltic have it too: seven pieces in four spellings.

Scott Joplin and a folk song from Liaoning

A march from 1896 and a Chinese folk song, the march in 4/4 and the song in 2/4 at half the values.

Coltrane and Beethoven

Coltrane's 3/4 on My Favorite Things is Beethoven's 3/8, note for note at half the values.

Charlie Parker and Monteverdi

A syncopated bebop phrase and a tenor line from a Venetian madrigal. Monteverdi writes it at twice the note values, over four bars, more than 350 years earlier.

Ties and beats

The same durations, grouped another way.

A tie or a single note, three beats or two: writers split the same durations differently, often to show where the beat falls. The sound doesn't change.

Wayne Shorter and Beethoven

Shorter's chain of tied eighths in 3/4 is Beethoven's quarters across the beat in 6/8. A good pair for teaching three against two.

The Carpenters and an Irish tune

One tie apart: where the bass plays a quarter, the tune writes two eighths tied together.

How matches are found

Proportions, not symbols.

For every excerpt, the app notes where each attack and each rest begins, as a fraction of the whole excerpt, with tied notes merged into one. Two excerpts with the same fractions are the same rhythm, whatever the time signature, the note values or the number of bars.

In the app, the source card under each excerpt counts the other pieces that write its rhythm another way, and names their time signatures. One tap shows every one of them side by side.

  • 7 pieces share the Now's the Time rhythm, in four spellings: Parker, Jingle Bells, Mozart, Beethoven and three folk songs.
  • 3 pieces have the Off the Wall bass rhythm: Michael Jackson's record and two Chinese folk songs.
