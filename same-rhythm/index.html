Same rhythm, different notation

One rhythm, written two ways.

A Bach chorale in 12/8 and a Miles Davis solo in 4/4 look nothing alike on the page. Tap them and they are the same rhythm. Actual Rhythms finds pairs like this across all 11,896 pieces, so every excerpt can show you its twins.

Same notes, another meter

Identical on the page, a different time signature.

Nothing changes but the meter. In 2/2 the beat is the half note; in 4/4 it is the quarter. Read them both and feel where the pulse moves.

Half or double the note values

Eighths become sixteenths, quarters become eighths.

Halve every note and a bar of 4/4 becomes a bar of 2/4. Double them and two bars become four. The rhythm stays the same; only how busy it looks on the page changes.

Ties, dots and rests

The same durations, grouped another way.

A tie or a dot, one rest or two: writers split the same durations differently, often to show where the beat falls. The sound doesn't change.

How pairs are found

Proportions, not symbols.

For every excerpt, the app notes where each attack and each rest begins, as a fraction of the whole excerpt, with tied notes merged into one. Two excerpts with the same fractions are the same rhythm, whatever the time signature, the note values or the number of bars.

In the app, the source card under each excerpt counts the other pieces that write its rhythm another way, and names their time signatures. One tap shows every one of them side by side.
