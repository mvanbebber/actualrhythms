Why real music

Practice the rhythms you'll actually be asked to read.

The right rhythms, in the right amounts

Generated exercises treat every combination as equally likely. Real music doesn't. Here, common figures come up often because composers use them often: two bars of even eighths in 2/4 turn up in 830 different pieces, while rare figures stay rare.

Every excerpt has a source

Each rhythm names its piece, composer, part, bars, place and date. One tap shows every other piece that uses it, including pieces that write it in another time signature with the same proportions.

Hear it as music

Listen plays the passage's own line on a sampled grand piano, or on bass, guitar or drums when that's the part it comes from. For about a third of the rhythms it can also play at the original tempo, from the score's metronome mark or the recording itself.

How it works

Read it, tap it, see every tap.

  1. Read

    Pick a level from steady beats to triplets and sixteenth syncopation, and the time signatures and styles you want.

  2. Tap

    After a one-bar count-in, tap the rhythm on the pad, or press Space, F or J. It plays twice through (four times for one-bar patterns).

  3. See

    Each pass gets its own lane under the staff: a gray tick where every note falls, a colored dot where you tapped. Your score and average timing come with it.

Three accuracy settings: Easy gives full credit within 110 ms of the note, Normal within 75 ms, Pro within 35 ms. Speakers and Bluetooth headphones each keep their own calibration.
